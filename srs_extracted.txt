1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the requirements for the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) of an affordable, AI-assisted WhatsApp automation platform for coaching institutes and education consultancies. It is the single reference for design, development, testing and pilot acceptance of the first release.

1.2 Scope

The MVP lets a coaching institute connect its official WhatsApp Business number, receive and answer student enquiries from a shared inbox, automate repetitive replies with rules and a knowledge-grounded AI assistant, capture student leads, run one consent-compliant follow-up workflow, book demo/counselling slots, and monitor usage, failures and outcomes from a dashboard. Subscription billing with enforced plan limits is included.

Everything not listed in Section 2.3 (In-scope) is out of scope for the MVP and is tracked in the post-MVP backlog.

1.6 Conventions

Requirement IDs follow GROUP-NN (for example WA-03). Each is uniquely testable.

Must = required for MVP release. Should = target for MVP, may slip to the first patch. Could = only if time remains. Won’t = explicitly excluded from MVP.

The word “shall” marks a binding requirement; “may” marks an option.

Numeric targets (limits, timings) are initial values and must be validated in pilots.

2. Product overview

2.1 Vision and positioning

Positioning: Affordable WhatsApp automation that any small business can set up and manage without a developer.

Differentiation rests on four pillars: (1) affordable, transparent pricing with clear limits; (2) guided onboarding; (3) ready-made coaching workflows (admission enquiry and demo booking); (4) reliability — failure logs, duplicate prevention, opt-out handling and human takeover. Basic chatbot capability is not a differentiator; ease of use, pricing clarity and complete workflow execution are.

2.5 Design constraints

Production messaging must not be assumed enabled until Meta business verification, number registration and template approvals are complete.

Only Meta-permitted messaging is allowed: business-initiated messages require prior opt-in and an approved template outside the 24-hour window.

AI shall only state fees, discounts, schedules and availability that the institute has entered and authorised.

Low-priced plans must not promise unlimited AI or unlimited messaging.

Small team (likely a single developer) — prefer managed services and simple architecture over custom infrastructure.

2.6 Assumptions and dependencies

Institutes own or can obtain a phone number eligible for the WhatsApp Business Platform and complete Meta business verification.

Meta APIs, pricing and policies may change; the integration is isolated behind an adapter so changes stay local.

Each institute pays Meta messaging charges directly through its own WABA payment method (to be confirmed, see OQ-01). The platform bills only its own subscription.

An LLM provider, email provider (transactional) and payment gateway are available with adequate SLAs.

3. User roles, permissions, and management layers

The platform enforces two completely distinct management layers: (1) Platform Management Console for AutoMask internal SaaS operations, and (2) Client Business Workspace for subscribing coaching institute operations.

Permissions are enforced on the server for every request. "Own" means only conversations or records assigned to the user.

3.1 Platform Management Console (AutoMask Internal Operations)
Exclusively for AutoMask platform administrators, support engineers, and billing operations.
- Platform Super Admin (`platform_admin`): Global tenant management, plan quotas, controlled suspension, platform staff access, system-wide audit logs.
- Support Operations (`platform_support`): Webhook failure monitoring, queue status, delivery errors, support tickets.
- Billing Operations (`platform_billing`): Subscription tiers, pricing, and quota overrides.
- SRS Line 615 Privacy Safeguard: Platform Admin access to customer message content is strictly disabled by default. Content access is only unlocked via an active, logged, time-boxed support grant issued by the workspace Owner.

3.2 Client Business Workspace (Tenant Operations)
Each subscribing business operates in an isolated workspace with its own Owner, Admins, Custom Roles, and Agents.
- Workspace Owner (`owner`): Complete administrative control over workspace, billing, plan upgrades, team roster, custom roles, permission overrides, and support grants.
- Workspace Admin (`admin`): Manages daily operations: workflows, automations, templates, broadcasts, contacts, and AI knowledge. Client Admins can NEVER access other client workspaces, modify platform subscription plans, or self-escalate privileges.
- Operations Manager (`manager`): Supervises counsellors, monitors conversations, schedules broadcasts, and manages demo slots.
- Counsellor / Worker (`agent`): Handles student chats, updates leads, and schedules demo slots.
- Custom Roles: Tailored roles with granular permissions configured by the Owner.

Security Boundaries:
- Platform-level authorization (`platform:*`) is strictly separated from workspace-level authorization (`workspace:*`).
- Multi-tenancy is enforced on the server and isolated via PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS).
- Client-supplied workspace IDs and role claims are never trusted; canonical resolution is performed via verified cryptographic JWT.
- Client Admins are strictly forbidden from modifying subscription billing, transferring workspace ownership, or granting themselves additional privileges.
- Platform Admins cannot read customer messages by default.
- All sensitive operations (suspensions, support grants, permission edits) are immutably logged in audit trails.

4. End-to-end workflows

4.1 Onboarding workflow

Owner signs up with email and password and verifies the email address.

System creates the workspace (workspace_id), owner membership and a default trial/Free plan.

Owner enters business profile: institute name, category, business hours, timezone, admin details.

Owner connects WhatsApp through Meta Embedded Signup; system stores encrypted tokens and subscribes the webhook.

Owner enters courses, fees, batch timings, FAQs and escalation rules (or imports from a template pack).

Owner enables the prebuilt coaching templates, sets booking slots and sends a test message.

Dashboard shows an onboarding checklist and status Connected only when the webhook and send test succeed.

4.2 Inbound message pipeline

Meta calls the webhook. The handler verifies the signature over the raw body, stores the raw event, enqueues a job and returns HTTP 200 immediately.

Worker validates the event and resolves workspace and WhatsApp number from phone_number_id.

Deduplicate by provider message ID (unique constraint). Duplicates are acknowledged and ignored.

Upsert contact and conversation; persist the message; update the 24-hour window timestamp.

Check opt-out: if the customer has opted out, process only the message record and any opt-in request; no automated reply.

Check human takeover: if active, notify the assignee and stop automation and AI.

Check usage limits and business hours; apply rule evaluation (Section 4.3).

Execute actions, send outbound messages, record execution result and usage events.

Push realtime update to the inbox; delivery status webhooks later update message state.

4.3 Response decision order

The router applies the first matching step and stops:

Order

Check

Outcome

1

Opted out or blocked contact

No automated outbound. Log only.

2

Human takeover active

Route to human inbox; AI paused.

3

Opt-out keyword or intent in the message

Record opt-out, send one confirmation, cancel pending follow-ups.

4

Active booking sub-flow for this contact

Continue the booking dialogue (slot selection/confirmation).

5

Matching automation rule (by priority)

Execute configured actions.

6

FAQ/knowledge match and AI quota available

Send grounded AI answer; capture lead details.

7

Sensitive, low-confidence or unknown query

Fallback message and human handoff.

4.4 Lead capture and follow-up workflow

On first inbound message the system creates a contact and a lead with status New.

AI/rules collect name, class, course interest and preferred batch; each field is validated and saved.

When interest is shown (intent or rule), status becomes Interested and the follow-up sequence starts if the booking is not completed.

Before every follow-up step the system checks opt-out, booking, recent reply, sequence completion, messaging window or template, business hours and duplicate job key.

A reply, booking, opt-out, takeover or terminal status (Converted/Lost) stops or updates the sequence.

4.5 Demo/counselling booking workflow

Booking intent is detected, or the student taps a booking button.

System queries real available slots and offers the next three to five choices as buttons or a list.

Student selects a slot; the system attempts to reserve it inside a database transaction.

Only if the reservation succeeds is the booking marked Confirmed; the student receives a confirmation and the counsellor is notified.

Reminders are scheduled; the lead status changes to Demo Booked.

If the slot was just taken, the student is shown the updated availability. The system never claims a booking that did not persist.

4.6 Human handoff workflow

Handoff is triggered by: sensitive topic (discount, refund, fee dispute, complaint), AI confidence below threshold, no knowledge match, student asks for a person, or repeated failure.

System sets the conversation to Needs Human, pauses AI/automation, sends a short holding message in business hours or a callback message after hours.

Assigned agent or all agents are notified in-app and by email.

Agent replies; the dashboard tracks time-to-first-human-reply. Agent may resume automation manually.

6.2 Integrity and security rules

Provider message IDs, booking idempotency keys, follow-up job keys and usage reference IDs shall have database uniqueness constraints.

Foreign keys and indexes shall support workspace-scoped lookups (workspace_id leading column).

Phone numbers are stored in E.164 format; free text fields are length-limited and sanitised on output.

Secrets (WhatsApp tokens, gateway keys) are encrypted or held in a secrets manager and excluded from logs and exports.

6.3 Retention, backup and deletion

Messages and leads are retained while the subscription is active; default message retention 12 months (configurable per plan).

After cancellation, data is kept 30 days for reactivation, then deleted or anonymised; Owner can request earlier deletion.

A contact’s data shall be deletable on request (right to erasure) while preserving anonymised usage counts.

Automated daily backups with point-in-time recovery where available; backup restore tested at least once before pilot launch.
